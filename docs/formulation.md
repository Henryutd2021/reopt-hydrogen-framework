# Formulation

The model chooses the sizes of the components of an integrated energy system (wind, PV, battery, electrolyzer, hydrogen tank, fuel cell and, optionally, a concentrating solar power (CSP) plant with thermal energy storage). It also schedules their operation hour by hour over one representative year. It minimizes the life-cycle cost of the system: capital and operation-and-maintenance (O&M) costs, net of the revenue from energy delivered to the grid, hydrogen sales and production incentives. All amounts are present values over the analysis period.

The model follows the REopt formulation family (NREL's techno-economic optimization model; Cutler et al., *REopt: A Platform for Energy System Integration and Optimization*, NREL/TP-7A40-70022, 2017). It extends that formulation with an electrolyzer, a hydrogen tank and a fuel cell.

- **Source.** The core model, Part A, is stated as in H. Li and J. Zhang, *J. Mech. Des.* 146 (2024) 020903, Eqs. (1)–(35). Equation numbers below refer to that article.
- **Options.** Part B describes options used in related studies of the series, written in the same notation.
- **Conventions.** [Conventions](#conventions-of-this-implementation) lists the definitions this implementation makes explicit.

Time steps are hours, $h \in \mathcal H = \{1, \dots, |\mathcal H|\}$, so a flow in kW equals an energy in kWh per step.

## Part A: core model (Eqs. 1–35)

| Symbol | Meaning | In this implementation |

| $\mathcal T^{h} \subset \mathcal T$ | technologies using hydrogen (fuel cells), index $f$ | fuel cell |
| $\mathcal T^{gh} \subset \mathcal T$ | hydrogen-producing technologies (electrolyzers), index $e$ | electrolyzer |
| $\mathcal T^{g} \subset \mathcal T$ | technologies with an electric output (generators) | wind, PV, fuel cell |

| $\delta^{d}_h$ | energy to be delivered to the grid | kW | series `delivery_target` |
| $\bar\delta^{g}_h$ | interconnection limit | kW | series `interconnection_limit` |

|---|---|---|---|

| $Z^{\sigma}_t$ | build indicator (binary) | – | `z_sigma` |
| $X^{bkW}_b$, $X^{bkWh}_b$ | storage power and energy capacity | kW, kWh | `bkW`, `bkWh` |

| $X^{pts}_{bth}$, $X^{ptg}_{th}$, $X^{pte}_{th}$, $X^{ptc}_{th}$ | production to storage, to the grid, to the electrolyzer, curtailed | kW | `pts`, `ptg`, `pte`, `ptc` |
| $X^{se}_{bh}$ | stored energy at the end of step $h$ ($h = 0$: initial) | kWh | `se` |
| $X^{dfs}_{bh}$ | power discharged from storage | kW | `dfs` |

| $X^{p}_{eh}$ | actual hydrogen production | kg/h | `p_e` |
| $X^{sell}_{eh}$ | hydrogen sold | kg | `sell` |
| $X^{etf}_{eh}$ | hydrogen to the fuel cell | kg | `etf` |
| $X^{ftg}_{fh}$ | power from the fuel cell | kW | expression |
| $X^{sh}_{th}$ | hydrogen in the tank at the end of step $h$ | kg | `sh` |
| $X^{pi}_t$ | production incentive collected (present value) | \$ | `X_pi_<tech>` |
| $Z^{pi}_t$ | incentive eligibility (binary) | – | `Z_pi_<tech>` |

### Objective (1)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min\;& \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\left(c^{cm}_t X^{\sigma}_t + c^{cb}_t Z^{\sigma}_t\right)
+ \sum_{b\in\mathcal B}\left(c^{kW}_b X^{bkW}_b + \left(c^{kWh}_b + c^{omb}\right) X^{bkWh}_b\right) \\
&+ \left(1-f^{tow}\right) f^{om}\left(\sum_{t\in\mathcal T} c^{om\sigma}_t X^{\sigma}_t + \sum_{t\in\mathcal T,\,h\in\mathcal H} c^{omp}_t X^{rp}_{th} + \sum_{h\in\mathcal H} c^{ome} X^{rp}_{eh}\right) \\
&- \left(1-f^{tow}\right) f^{e}\left(\sum_{h\in\mathcal H}\Big(c^{e}_h X^{stg}_h + \sum_{t\in\mathcal T} c^{e}_h X^{ptg}_{th}\Big) + \sum_{h\in\mathcal H} c^{hts}_h X^{sell}_{eh}\right)
- \left(1-f^{tow}\right)\sum_{t\in\mathcal T} X^{pi}_t
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
$$

The terms are, in order: generating-technology capital costs, storage capital costs, fixed and variable O&M costs (including electrolyzer O&M), the energy export payment and hydrogen profit, and production incentives.

### Generation and storage

$$X^{rp}_{th} \le \bar b^{\sigma}_t Z^{to}_{th} \qquad \forall t\in\mathcal T,\ h\in\mathcal H \tag{2}$$

$$X^{se}_{b,0} = w^{0}_b X^{bkWh}_b \qquad \forall b \tag{3}$$

$$\underline w^{bkWh}_b \le X^{bkWh}_b \le \bar w^{bkWh}_b \qquad \forall b \tag{4}$$

$$\underline w^{bkW}_b \le X^{bkW}_b \le \bar w^{bkW}_b \qquad \forall b \tag{5}$$

$$X^{se}_{bh} = X^{se}_{b,h-1} + \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\eta^{+}_{bt} X^{pts}_{bth} - X^{dfs}_{bh}/\eta^{-}_b \qquad \forall b,\ h \tag{6}$$

$$X^{se}_{bh} \ge w^{mcp}_b X^{bkWh}_b \qquad \forall b,\ h \tag{7}$$

$$X^{bkW}_b \ge \sum_{t\in\mathcal T_b} X^{pts}_{bth} + X^{dfs}_{bh} \qquad \forall b,\ h \tag{8}$$

$$X^{bkWh}_b \ge X^{se}_{bh} \qquad \forall b,\ h \tag{9}$$

### Production incentives

$$X^{pi}_t \le \min\Big\{ M Z^{pi}_t,\ \sum_{h\in\mathcal H} \bar\imath_t f^{pi}_t f^{p}_{th} f^{li}_t X^{rp}_{th} \Big\} \qquad \forall t \tag{10}$$

$$X^{\sigma}_t \le \bar\imath^{\sigma}_t + \bar b^{\sigma}_t\left(1 - Z^{pi}_t\right) \qquad \forall t \tag{11}$$

### Electrolyzer, hydrogen tank and fuel cell

$$f^{p}_{eh} f^{1}_e X^{rp}_{eh} = X^{p}_{eh} \qquad \forall h \tag{12}$$

$$m_e X^{p}_{eh} = \sum_{t\in\mathcal T} X^{pte}_{th} + \sum_{b\in\mathcal B} X^{ste}_{bh} \qquad \forall h \tag{13}$$

$$X^{rp}_{eh} \le X^{kg}_e \qquad \forall h \tag{14}$$

$$X^{sh}_{th} = X^{sh}_{t,h-1} + X^{p}_{eh} - X^{sell}_{eh} - X^{etf}_{eh} \qquad \forall h \tag{15}$$

$$X^{sh}_{th} \le X^{tank} \qquad \forall h \tag{16}$$

$$f^{p}_{fh} f^{1}_f X^{rp}_{fh}\, m_f = X^{etf}_{eh} \qquad \forall f\in\mathcal T^{h},\ h \tag{17}$$

$$f^{p}_{fh} f^{1}_f X^{rp}_{fh} = X^{ftg}_{fh} \qquad \forall f\in\mathcal T^{h},\ h \tag{18}$$

$$X^{rp}_{fh} \le X^{kW}_f \qquad \forall h \tag{19}$$

### Energy balance and delivery to the grid

$$\sum_{t\in\mathcal T} f^{p}_{th} f^{1}_t X^{rp}_{th} + \sum_{b\in\mathcal B} X^{dfs}_{bh}
= \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\Big(\sum_{b\in\mathcal B} X^{pts}_{bth} + X^{ptg}_{th} + X^{pte}_{th} + X^{ptc}_{th}\Big) + \sum_{b\in\mathcal B}\left(X^{ste}_{bh} + X^{stg}_{bh}\right) \qquad \forall h \tag{20}$$

$$\sum_{t\in\mathcal T} X^{ptg}_{th} + \sum_{b\in\mathcal B} X^{stg}_{bh} = \delta^{d}_h \qquad \forall h \tag{21}$$

$$\sum_{t\in\mathcal T} f^{p}_{th} f^{1}_t X^{rp}_{th} = \sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\Big(\sum_{b\in\mathcal B} X^{pts}_{bth} + X^{ptg}_{th} + X^{pte}_{th} + X^{ptc}_{th}\Big) \qquad \forall h \tag{22}$$

$$\sum_{b\in\mathcal B} X^{stg}_{bh} + \sum_{t\in\mathcal T} X^{ptg}_{th} \le \bar\delta^{g}_h \qquad \forall h \tag{23}$$

**Scope of the sums.**

- **Generators only** (wind, PV, fuel cell; the electric flows, in kW): the sums over $t$ in Eqs. (6), (8), (13), (20)–(23), in the energy export payment and in the variable O&M term $c^{omp}_t X^{rp}_{th}$ of Eq. (1) run over the generators $\mathcal T^{g}$.
- **Electrolyzer:** its O&M is the separate term $c^{ome} X^{rp}_{eh}$ of Eq. (1). Eqs. (2) and (10)–(11) apply to the generators and to the electrolyzer, whose rated production is in kg/h.
- **Tank:** it has no production.
- **All technologies:** the capital and fixed O&M terms of Eq. (1) run over all of them.

Eq. (20) balances generation and storage discharge against their uses. Eq. (21) sets the energy delivered to the grid, the stabilized output of the system, to $\delta^{d}_h$. Eq. (22) allocates generation to its uses. Eq. (23) limits delivery to the interconnection capacity.

### Domains (24)–(35)

All sizes, flows, storage levels and incentives are non-negative (24)–(33); $Z^{to}_{th} \in \{0,1\}$ (34) and $Z^{pi}_t \in \{0,1\}$ (35).

## Conventions of this implementation

- **Units:** kW, kWh, kg, kg/h, kWh_th and \$. `parameters.csv` must state the units of `parameters.canonical_units()`; values in other units are refused, not converted.
- **Sizes in Eq. (1):**
  - The capital and fixed O&M terms over $\mathcal T$ include the electrolyzer and the tank, with $X^{\sigma}_e = X^{kg}_e$ and $X^{\sigma}_{tank} = X^{tank}$.
  - The fuel-cell size $X^{kW}_f$ is its $X^{\sigma}_f$.
  - Battery power capacity is $X^{bkW}_b$ throughout.
- **Allocation by generator:** each generator's production is allocated to its own flows, $f^{p}_{th} f^{1}_t X^{rp}_{th} = \sum_b X^{pts}_{bth} + X^{ptg}_{th} + X^{pte}_{th} + X^{ptc}_{th}$ for every $t \in \mathcal T^{g}$ (`allocation_by_generator`). Summed over $t$, this gives Eq. (22). It makes the flows of each technology, such as its curtailment, well defined.
- **Size and production:** rated production does not exceed the size, $X^{rp}_{th} \le X^{\sigma}_t$ for every generator (`rated_production_le_size`). Eqs. (14) and (19) state this for the electrolyzer and the fuel cell.
- **Build indicator:** $Z^{\sigma}_t$ is binary, with $X^{\sigma}_t \le \bar b^{\sigma}_t Z^{\sigma}_t$ (`build_indicator`), so the y-intercept $c^{cb}_t$ is paid only when $t$ is built. It is created only for generators with $c^{cb}_t > 0$.
- **Electrolyzer O&M:** $c^{ome}$ multiplies the rated production summed over the hours.
- **Present-worth factors:**
  - $f^{om} = \mathrm{pwf}(N, d, e^{om})$ and $f^{e} = \mathrm{pwf}(N, d, e^{e})$, with $\mathrm{pwf}(N,d,e) = \sum_{y=1}^{N}\left(\frac{1+e}{1+d}\right)^y$.
  - $f^{pi}_t = \mathrm{pwf}(\min(N_t^{pi}, N), d, 0)$.
  - $c^{e}_h$ and $c^{hts}_h$ escalate together at $e^{e}$.
- **Capital costs:** $c^{cm}_t$ is the cost after investment incentives. `effective_capital_cost(capex, tax_rate, discount_rate, itc, macrs)` computes it from an investment tax credit and a MACRS schedule: capex × (1 − ITC) minus the present value of the depreciation tax shield, on a basis reduced by half the ITC.
- **Horizon:**
  - The articles model one year of 8,760 hours.
  - For a shorter horizon (examples, tests), the hourly sums in Eq. (1), and in the bound of Eq. (10), are multiplied by $W = 8760/|\mathcal H|$, so that they remain annual next to the capital costs (`Instance.hour_weight`). For a full year, $W = 1$.
- **Big number $M$ in Eq. (10):** the largest incentive that the size bound allows, $\bar\imath_t f^{pi}_t f^{li}_t W \sum_h f^{p}_{th} \bar b^{\sigma}_t$.
- **Production factors:** $f^{p}_{eh} = 1$ for the electrolyzer and $f^{p}_{fh} = 1$ for the fuel cell. Wind and PV take theirs from the series; downtime can be represented by scaling them (`preprocess.apply_availability`).
- **Hydrogen tank:**
  - The tank starts at `tank.initial_fill` × $X^{tank}$ (default 0, `tank_initial_level`).
  - Without a tank in the configuration, $X^{sh} = 0$ and Eq. (15) reads $X^{p}_{eh} = X^{sell}_{eh} + X^{etf}_{eh}$.
- **One battery, one electrolyzer, one fuel cell:** $\mathcal B$, $\mathcal T^{gh}$ and $\mathcal T^{h}$ each have at most one element, and $\eta^{+}_{bt}$ is the same for all $t$.
- **$Z^{to}_{th}$:** it enters only Eq. (2). `ModelOptions(on_off="fixed_on")` fixes it to 1 and gives the same optimum with a smaller model; `"binary"` (default) keeps Eq. (34).

## Modelling notes

- The model is deterministic: one year of hourly data stands for every year of the analysis period, escalated by the present-worth factors (perfect foresight).
- Storage levels have an initial condition (Eq. 3; tank and thermal storage as above) and no end-of-horizon condition. On a short horizon, an initial level above the minimum is usable energy; the synthetic example therefore starts the battery at its minimum state of charge and the tank and thermal storage empty.
- Eq. (21) is an equality: if the system cannot deliver $\delta^{d}_h$ in some hour, the model is infeasible. The shortfall extension below makes such cases solvable at a penalty.

## Part B: options used in related studies

These options are written in the notation of Part A. Each is off unless selected in `ModelOptions` or in the configuration.

### B1. Site load with grid purchases and outages (`delivery="site_load"`)

The system serves a site electric load $L_h$, as in REopt's site-specific setting with an electricity tariff and grid outages. The changes to Part A:

- **Eq. (21) is replaced** by a load balance. Production to load $X^{ptl}_{th}$, storage to load $X^{stl}_{bh}$ and grid purchases $X^{gl}_h$ serve the load:
  $$\sum_t X^{ptl}_{th} + \sum_b X^{stl}_{bh} + X^{gl}_h = L_h$$
  (`site_load_balance`).
- **Allocations:** $X^{ptl}_{th}$ joins the allocations of Eqs. (20) and (22), and $X^{stl}_{bh}$ joins the storage outflows of Eq. (20).
- **Grid to electrolyzer:** grid electricity $X^{gte}_h$ may also supply the electrolyzer; it is added to the right-hand side of Eq. (13).
- **Purchase cost:** purchases cost $c^{p}_h$ (series `purchase_price`). The objective adds $(1-f^{tow}) f^{e} \sum_h c^{p}_h (X^{gl}_h + X^{gte}_h)$.
- **Outage hours** (series `outage` = 1): $X^{gl}_h = X^{gte}_h = 0$, and the limit of Eq. (23) is 0.
- **Exports** remain optional, up to $\bar\delta^{g}_h$.

### B2. Constant-rate electrolysis (`electrolyzer_operation="constant"`)

$X^{rp}_{eh} = X^{kg}_e$ for every $h$ (`constant_rate_electrolysis`): the electrolyzer runs at its capacity in every hour. The default, `"variable"`, allows any rate up to the capacity, Eq. (14).

### B3. CSP plant with thermal storage supplying the electrolyzer with heat (`csp=True`, `electrolyzer_supply="csp_heat"`)

A CSP plant (solar field, receiver and molten-salt storage) of design thermal output $X^{csp}$ (kW_th) delivers $f^{csp}_h X^{csp}$ kWh_th in step $h$. The series `csp_thermal_cf`, $f^{csp}_h$, is computed outside the model, e.g. with NREL's System Advisor Model (SAM, through PySAM) from NSRDB weather for the site.

The heat goes directly to the electrolyzer ($Q^{dir}_h$), into thermal storage ($Q^{ch}_h$), or is left unused ($Q^{dump}_h$):

$$f^{csp}_h X^{csp} = Q^{dir}_h + Q^{ch}_h + Q^{dump}_h \qquad \text{(csp\_heat\_balance)}$$

$$S_h = S_{h-1} + \eta^{+}_{tes} Q^{ch}_h - Q^{dis}_h/\eta^{-}_{tes},\quad S_h \le X^{tes},\quad S_0 = w^0_{tes} X^{tes} \qquad \text{(tes\_balance, tes\_capacity, tes\_initial)}$$

The electrolyzer draws all of its energy as heat from the plant. Its heat demand per kg is $m^{th}_e + r^{csp} m_e$:

- $m^{th}_e$ is its thermal consumption (`electrolyzer.heat_kwh_per_kg`);
- $r^{csp}$ (`csp.heat_per_kwh_e`) is the heat drawn per kWh of its electricity requirement.

This replaces Eq. (13):

$$\left(m^{th}_e + r^{csp} m_e\right) X^{p}_{eh} = Q^{dir}_h + Q^{dis}_h \qquad \text{(electrolyzer\_heat\_supply)}$$

The plant's parasitic electricity, $\pi_h X^{csp}$ (series `csp_parasitic`, kWh per kW_th), is bought from the grid at $c^{p}_h$ and reported with the grid purchases. Outage hours are therefore not supported together with the CSP plant: with `delivery="site_load"` and a positive outage series, the model refuses a CSP configuration that has a parasitic load. Capital and fixed O&M costs of the plant and the storage enter Eq. (1) like those of the other technologies.

### B4. Shortfall (extension; `shortfall_penalty`)

An optional extension. A non-negative shortfall $X^{sf}_h$ is added to the left-hand side of Eq. (21), at a price of `shortfall_penalty` \$/kWh in the objective (with factor $f^{e}$). This is useful for screening inputs before the delivery target is final.

## Indicators (computed after the solve; `results.summarize`)

- **Annual quantities:** hourly sums × $W$.
- **Available and unused wind and PV energy:**
  - Available energy is $f^{p}_{th} f^{1}_t X^{\sigma}_t$, summed over wind and PV.
  - Unused energy is the available energy minus the generation that is used, i.e. $f^{p}_{th} f^{1}_t X^{rp}_{th} - X^{ptc}_{th}$. It includes both rated production below the size and explicit curtailment $X^{ptc}$.
  - The indicator is their ratio. The explicit curtailment $X^{ptc}$ is also reported on its own.
- **Electrolyzer capacity factor:** annual production / (8760 × $X^{kg}_e$).
- **Levelized cost of hydrogen (LCOH)**, as defined in this framework:
  $$\mathrm{LCOH} = \frac{C^{cap}_{H_2} + f^{om} C^{om}_{H_2} + f^{e} C^{el}_{H_2}}{\mathrm{pwf}(N, d, 0)\cdot \text{annual hydrogen production}}$$
  - $C^{cap}_{H_2}$: capital cost of the electrolyzer and tank, and of the CSP plant and thermal storage when they supply the electrolyzer.
  - $C^{om}_{H_2}$: their fixed O&M and the electrolyzer's variable O&M per year.
  - $C^{el}_{H_2}$: the yearly cost of the electricity the electrolyzer uses. On-site electricity is valued at `electricity_value`: the export price $c^e_h$ (default), zero, or a fixed price. Grid electricity and CSP parasitics are valued at $c^{p}_h$.
  - Taxes and incentives are not included.
- **LCOE of wind or PV generation:** capital costs (including the y-intercept $c^{cb}_t$ when built) and O&M costs of the generator, over $\mathrm{pwf}(N, d, 0)$ × annual generation.
- **Cost terms:** capital, O&M, revenue, incentives and purchases, as they enter Eq. (1). They add up to the objective.
